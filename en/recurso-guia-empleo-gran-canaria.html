2The most used job sites and apps in Gran Canaria

InfoJobs

The site with the most job offers on the island, especially in hospitality, retail, warehousing, cleaning and health and social care. You sign up for free, upload your CV and apply for offers. Website: infojobs.net. We explain how to sign up and complete your profile.

Indeed

It collects offers from many websites, including companies' own sites. Search by job and municipality, for example «camarero/a Maspalomas» (waiter Maspalomas) or «limpieza Telde» (cleaning Telde), and turn on email alerts. Website: es.indeed.com.

Glassdoor

It has many offers from the island's supermarkets, hotels and shops, and reviews from people who have worked at each company. Website: glassdoor.es. Here is our Glassdoor guide.

LinkedIn

Professional network with job offers. Useful above all for office work, administration, tourism, the social sector and technology, and so that companies can find you. Website: linkedin.com/jobs. Our LinkedIn guide helps you create your profile.

Job Today

Mobile app with quick offers in hospitality, retail and customer service, many in Las Palmas de Gran Canaria and the south. You can chat with the company. Website: jobtoday.com.

Hacesfalta.org

Job and volunteering site for NGOs and social organisations. Very useful if you are looking for work in the social sector: social work, social education, integration, care. Website: hacesfalta.org.

Other general job sites

  • Infoempleo, with its own app.
  • Universia, with jobs and internships for students and recent graduates.
  • Buscojobs.
  • Construyendo Empleo, for the construction sector.
  • Womenalia, a professional network for women, and beBee, a professional network with job offers.

Job search engines

They gather offers from many websites at once: Jooble, Iberempleos, Jobijoba, Opcionempleo and Adzuna.

3Public employment services

Canary Islands Employment Service (SCE)

Registering as a jobseeker is free and gives you access to job offers, guidance and many courses, because quite a few free training courses require you to be registered.

  • Website of the Canary Islands Employment Service
  • How to register as a jobseeker
  • Job applications and offers
  • SCE job search, which you can filter by island and municipality
  • SCE apps for Android (direct download): appointments, your registration and job offers from your phone
  • Create your CV with the SCE
  • Working in Europe (EURES) at the SCE
  • Career guidance

We also explain it in our guide Looking for work in the Canary Islands?.

Some tips so that the SCE takes you into account when job offers come in:

  • Book an appointment through the online office, the SCE app or by calling 012 (928 301 012).
  • Choose your occupations carefully. You can list up to 6. The SCE looks for candidates through them, so they must match what you can do.
  • Extend your search area to the whole island of Gran Canaria if you can travel. You ask for this at your employment office.
  • Keep your registration up to date and hand in the certificates of the courses you take.
  • Ask for the employment plans and the PFAE. The SCE's agreements with town councils and organisations often hire people for cleaning, labouring, gardening or maintenance. PFAE (training programmes combined with employment) last about 11 or 12 months: you work with a contract while you train, and at the end you get a professional certificate. Both applications are valid for one year; after that you have to apply again.
  • Apply for the «Servicios para el Empleo» if they apply to you, always before you are hired, because they give support to the company that hires you: Certifícate (if you have a professional certificate), Retorno al Empleo (if you have been registered for 12 months or more) and Incentívate (aged 16 to 29, with Youth Guarantee). You apply on the SCE website.

Youth Guarantee (if you are under 30)

If you are between 16 and 29 and not working or studying, you can register for the Youth Guarantee (Garantía Juvenil) to access job offers, internships and training. Information on the SEPE website and at Gran Canaria Joven, from the island council (Cabildo). Other resources for young people:

  • Activa Joven Canarias, the SCE's site for young people.
  • Talento Joven from the Chamber of Commerce, with guidance and training for young people.

4Public sector jobs

Public administrations also hire people. The Government of the Canary Islands publishes its processes on Access to the Civil Service, where you can see the current calls for applications. Other public job pages:

  • Gran Canaria island council (Cabildo): public jobs
  • Las Palmas de Gran Canaria Town Council: current calls
  • Central State Administration: public job search, with vacancies all over Spain, including the Canary Islands, and a weekly public jobs bulletin you can receive by email.
  • Cabildo public companies that publish their own offers: Valora Gestión Tributaria, SODETEGC and the Gran Canaria Economic Promotion Company (SPEGC).
  • Gran Canaria island council – reserve lists: public jobs and reserve lists
  • Official gazettes, where all calls are published: Official State Gazette (BOE), Official Gazette of the Canary Islands (BOC) and Official Gazette of the Province of Las Palmas (BOP).

The other town councils publish their calls on their online offices and in the BOP.

An important point: to be a civil servant (funcionario/a), in most cases you need Spanish nationality or that of a European Union country. But foreign nationals legally resident in Spain can apply for contract staff (personal laboral) positions in public administrations on equal terms with Spanish nationals. This is set out in article 57 of the Basic Statute of Public Employees.

9Your rights while you look for work and when you start

  • All workers, with or without papers, have the right to be paid at least the minimum wage, to safety at work, to working hours and rest, and not to be discriminated against.
  • When you start working, the company must register you with Social Security from the first day. If you don't have a Social Security number, you can request it online from Social Security.
  • If you don't have permission to work, you cannot work legally, but you can train in sectors with job prospects, volunteer to practise the language and meet people, and prepare for regularisation. If you are already working without papers, you have the same employment rights and you can report abuse.
  • If your rights are violated, you can report it to the Labour and Social Security Inspectorate in Las Palmas: Luis Doreste Silva, 64 · 928 291 464 / 928 291 656 · itpalmas@mites.gob.es. You can also ask a trade union or an NGO for help.
  • If you work as a hotel room attendant, the Las Kellys Association advises and supports you: 603 038 344.
  • If you work in domestic work or care, you have the right to a contract, registration from the first day, at least the minimum wage in proportion to your hours, and 30 days of holiday a year.

15How to spot a fake job offer

Fake job offers also circulate online. Some are after your money and others after your personal data. Be suspicious if:

  • They ask you for money to work: for a course, materials, a uniform or «administration fees». A real company does not charge you to hire you.
  • They hire you without an interview or offer you a job you had not applied for.
  • The salary is very high for an easy job, with no experience and from home.
  • They ask you to call numbers starting with 803, 806, 807, 905 or 907, which are premium-rate.
  • You can't find the company online or they write to you from a free email address such as Gmail on behalf of a large company.
  • They ask for your bank details or photos of your ID before you have signed anything.
  • The offer doesn't give the company's name or the municipality and only gives a number to message «privately».

If an offer looks suspicious, don't send money or data, search online for the company's name and a few phrases from the ad, and warn the page where you saw it. Spain's National Cybersecurity Institute (INCIBE) explains more warning signs on its page about fake job offers.
