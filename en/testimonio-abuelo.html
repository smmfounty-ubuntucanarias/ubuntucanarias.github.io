Testimony

My grandfather, my teacher of interculturality

By Soraya Mohamedi
Chairperson of Ubuntu Canarias · In memory of her grandfather

Voices of the World: stories in the first person

At Ubuntu Canarias we know that interculturality isn't always learned from books. Often, it's inherited from those who lived it before us.

That's why we created Voices of the World: a space where those who have lived that experience — or, as in today's case, received it up close — can share it in their own words, without intermediaries, to build bridges between cultures.

No single story speaks for all the others. Each testimony is unique, with its own context and lessons, and it is precisely that plurality of voices that gives this project its meaning.

Today we share the testimony of Soraya Mohamedi, chairperson of Ubuntu Canarias, who tells us about her grandfather and everything he taught her about respect, faith and living between cultures.

A perspective to keep building bridges

At Ubuntu Canarias we believe that testimonies like Soraya's remind us that respect between cultures and religions isn't always taught through speeches: it's often passed down by example, from one generation to the next.

Her story reflects something we hold as an association: that interculturality isn't a theory, but a daily practice, made up of small gestures of consideration toward those who think, believe or live differently.

Listening to these voices invites us to look at coexistence between cultures and religions through mutual respect and knowledge of the other — just as Soraya's grandfather lived it, without ever needing to name it.

Editorial note. This testimony has been published with the authorisation of its author, Soraya Mohamedi, chairperson of Ubuntu Canarias, in memory of her grandfather. The content reflects exclusively the personal experience and opinions of the person who signs it.
