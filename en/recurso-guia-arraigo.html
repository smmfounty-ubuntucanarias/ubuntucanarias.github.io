Arraigo explained simply

Ubuntu Canarias · 15 min read · Published on 27 November 2026

Have you been living in Spain without papers for some time? Do you want to know if you can apply for arraigo and what you need to prepare? This guide explains it step by step, in simple words.

Are you a professional or do you work for an organisation? We have a technical version of this guide: Arraigo: a guide for professionals.

Before you start, remember three things:

  • Reaching the required time does not mean you will get the permit automatically. You also have to meet the requirements.
  • The official information is in the Ministry's information sheets. If something in this guide does not match them, what the Ministry says is what counts.
  • The rules can change. This guide was reviewed on 27 November 2026.

1. Is arraigo my only option?

Not always. Other permits may suit you better, for example if:

  • Your family has Spanish nationality.
  • Your family is from a European Union country.
  • There are humanitarian reasons in your case.
  • You are a victim of gender-based violence or human trafficking.
  • You have cooperated with the police or other authorities.

Some of these permits do not require you to wait 2 years. Before you wait, ask someone to review your case.

2. What is arraigo?

It is a permit to live and work in Spain. It is given to people who already live here and have a life in Spain: family, work, studies or taking part in their community. You do not have to go back to your country to apply.

There are 5 types:

  1. Second-chance arraigo (segunda oportunidad).
  2. Social-employment arraigo (sociolaboral).
  3. Social arraigo (social).
  4. Social-training arraigo (socioformativo).
  5. Family arraigo (familiar).

Almost all of them last 1 year. Family arraigo lasts 5 years.

If you want to read the law: Immigration Regulation (BOE, in Spanish).

3. Can I apply?

You cannot apply for arraigo if:

  • You are from a European Union country, the European Economic Area or Switzerland.
  • You are a family member of a European citizen and the permit for family members of Europeans applies to you.
  • You already have another stay or residence permit, or another permit procedure is in progress.
  • You have an asylum application in progress.

Also:

  • You must be in Spain.
  • You cannot have a criminal record in Spain or in the countries where you lived during the 5 years before you arrived.
  • You cannot be a danger to public order, security or public health.
  • You cannot be banned from entering countries that have an agreement with Spain.
  • If you went back to your country with a return programme and signed a commitment not to come back for some time, that time must have passed.
  • You have to pay the fee.

4. How long do I need to have been in Spain?

Usually, 2 years in a row right before you apply.

  • During those 2 years you cannot be outside Spain for more than 90 days in total. All trips are added together. This rule is in the SEM Instructions 1/2025.
  • If you applied for asylum, the time your application was being processed does not count.
  • Family arraigo does not require 2 years.

If you have ever applied for asylum, ask someone to review your case before you do anything.

5. The 5 types of arraigo, one by one

A. Second-chance arraigo

It is for you if:

  • You had a residence permit in the last 2 years.
  • That permit was not an arraigo permit or another permit for exceptional circumstances.
  • You could not renew it, and the reason was not public order, security or public health.

There are also special cases if you had a trial that ended without a conviction. In that case, get legal advice.

Papers you need:

  • The decision that granted your previous permit.
  • Papers that show you had that permit and when it was valid.
  • Papers explaining why it was not renewed, if you have them.

More information: Ministry sheet 27.

B. Social-employment arraigo (sociolaboral)

It is for you if you have a job offer.

You need:

  • 2 years in Spain.
  • One or more contracts.
  • Together, at least 20 hours a week.
  • A salary equal to or higher than the minimum wage or the collective agreement salary, depending on the hours.

You can have several contracts if they are seasonal jobs or if you work for several employers at the same time.

The company must be up to date with the tax office and Social Security, and have enough money to pay you.

Two very important things:

  • When the Immigration Office accepts your application for processing (admisión a trámite), you can already work while you wait for the answer.
  • If you get the permit, you have 1 month for the company to register you with Social Security. If you are not registered, the permit is not valid.

Papers you need:

  • The contract or contracts signed by you and the company.
  • The company's tax number (NIF).
  • The company's deeds, if it is necessary to show who is signing.
  • Papers showing the company has money, for example its tax returns or its employment record.
  • Your qualification or training, if the job requires it. If it is a regulated profession, your recognised (homologated) qualification.

Do not confuse it with the 2026 regularisation. Having contracts of more than 90 days in a year was part of that process, not of this arraigo.

More information: Ministry sheet 29.

C. Social arraigo

There are 2 paths.

Path 1: you have family with papers in Spain

  • Your husband, your wife or your registered partner.
  • Or your father, your mother, your son or your daughter.

On this path you have to show money. You need 100% of the IPREM and your family member another 100%. In total, 200% of the IPREM. The money must be in Spain.

Path 2: you do not have family with papers

You need an integration report. In the Canary Islands, it is issued by the Government of the Canary Islands. The report looks at:

  • How long you have lived in your home.
  • Whether you have money to live on.
  • Whether you have taken part in programmes to find work.
  • Whether you are learning Spanish.
  • Whether you do courses and take part in activities.

Taking part in associations and NGOs helps

Helping an association or an NGO, going to its activities, volunteering or becoming a member can help you in the report. All foreign people can join an association, even without papers. Do not wait until you have papers to start.

Ask each association for a certificate with:

  • Your full name.
  • The name of the association.
  • What you have done.
  • The dates.
  • The hours, if they can be counted.
  • The signature and details of the association.

These certificates help, but they do not guarantee a positive report. The sooner you start, the more time you will be able to show.

Papers you need:

  • Marriage or registered partnership certificate, if this applies to you.
  • Birth certificates or other papers that show the family relationship.
  • Papers showing that your family member has a residence permit.
  • Papers about the money.
  • The integration report, if you do not have family.

More information: Ministry sheet 28.

D. Social-training arraigo (socioformativo)

It is for you if you are going to study.

You need:

  • 2 years in Spain.
  • To be enrolled in a course that is accepted.
  • The integration report.

Careful: not every course is accepted. These are:

  • Upper secondary school (Bachillerato) or full-time Vocational Training, in an official centre.
  • Complete professional certificates, level 1, 2 or 3.
  • Adult education, in person.
  • Courses from the employment service for jobs where there are not enough workers.

Some studies can be partly online, but with a minimum part in class.

Important dates:

  • If the course has an enrolment period, apply for arraigo in the 2 months before that period starts.
  • If you get the permit and did not have the enrolment yet, you have 3 months to present it.
  • If you do not present the enrolment on time or do not do the course, the permit can be taken away.

With this permit you can work up to 30 hours a week, with a salary equal to or higher than the minimum wage or the collective agreement salary, depending on the hours.

Papers you need:

  • The integration report.
  • The enrolment, the admission or a paper showing that you are doing the course.
  • If it is an employment service course, your written commitment to do it.

Before you enrol, check that your course is accepted.

More information: Ministry sheet 30.

E. Family arraigo

You do not need 2 years in Spain.

It is for you if:

  • You are the mother, father or guardian of a child with the nationality of a European Union country, the European Economic Area or Switzerland, who lives in Spain. You must live with the child and take care of them, or meet your obligations as a parent.
  • Or you take care of a family member with a disability who has that nationality, lives with you and depends on you.

Papers you need:

  • The child's birth certificate.
  • A paper showing the child's nationality.
  • The guardianship document, if you are the guardian.
  • If you care for a person with a disability: papers about the disability, that you are family, that they depend on you and that you live together.
  • The identity document of the European person.

More information: Ministry sheet 31.

6. The papers you always need

Prepare a folder with:

  1. Your passport. Copy every page.
  2. Papers showing how long you have been in Spain: the historical register certificate (padrón histórico, the most important one), papers from the doctor or hospital, course enrolments and certificates, social services papers and any official paper with your name and the date. Papers from the Spanish authorities are best.
  3. The criminal record certificate from the countries where you lived during the 5 years before you came to Spain. It must have an apostille or be legalised and, if it is not in Spanish, be translated by a sworn translator.
  4. The papers for your type of arraigo: the contract, the enrolment, your family papers or the integration report.
  5. The EX-10 form and the fee payment (form 790, code 052, section 2.3.1). The fee is usually paid within 10 working days after you submit the application.

About the criminal record, sometimes you do not need to present it:

  • If you have been in Spain for 5 years in a row, you do not need the one from your country.
  • If you already presented it in another procedure in the last 5 years and have not left Spain, you do not need to present it again.

The Immigration Office checks your Spanish record directly. If you have a criminal record, a trial or a problem with the police, get legal advice before you submit anything.

7. Where do I submit it?

  • At the Immigration Office (Oficina de Extranjería) of your province, with an appointment.
  • Or online, on the Mercurio website, if you have a digital certificate or an authorised person helps you.

Useful links (in Spanish):

  • Mercurio: apply online
  • Immigration appointments
  • Immigration procedures
  • Official forms
  • Fee 052

8. After you submit

If a paper is missing

The Immigration Office can ask you to hand it in within a deadline. If you do not answer correctly and on time, your file can be closed. If you do not understand what they are asking for, get help straight away.

While you wait

  • The Immigration Office has 3 months to answer.
  • If they do not tell you anything in 3 months, the answer is NO. You can appeal. Get help.
  • Check how your file is progressing.
  • Check your online notifications, if you chose to receive them that way. If you do not open a notification within 10 days, it counts as rejected.
  • If they cannot find you, the notification may be published in the Tablón Edictal Único (official notice board).

If they say yes

  • You have 1 month to apply for your card (TIE) at the police station.
  • If it is social-employment arraigo, you have 1 month to be registered with Social Security.

Can I work?

Yes, in any job and anywhere in Spain, as an employee or self-employed. With social-training arraigo, up to 30 hours a week. With social-employment arraigo, you can start working while you wait.

9. What happens when the year ends?

  • You can ask for another year (extension). Usually you have to show that you are looking for work and be registered with the employment service. There are exceptions if you are ill, have a disability or have reached retirement age.
  • With social-training arraigo, you have to continue studying. If you have finished, you present your qualification and show that you are looking for work.
  • You can also change to a residence and work permit, if you meet the requirements.

Start preparing a few months before it expires.

10. In the Canary Islands

The integration report

It is issued by the Government of the Canary Islands. It usually asks for courses on the Constitution, human rights, democracy and equality, with some exceptions. It also values:

  • Learning Spanish.
  • Taking part in programmes to find work and in cultural activities.
  • Volunteering.
  • Taking part in associations.

Check on their website how many hours of training they ask for, the exceptions and how to apply now (in Spanish):

  • Government of the Canary Islands: integration reports
  • Integration effort report
  • Social arraigo report. This page shows an old deadline; check the general reports page to see how to apply now.

The Immigration Office in Las Palmas

Plaza de la Feria, 24, 35003, Las Palmas de Gran Canaria. See the official page. Before you go, check whether you need an appointment.

11. Start keeping your papers today

  • Keep your padrón (municipal register) up to date.
  • Keep papers from the doctor and from other procedures.
  • Keep course enrolments and certificates.
  • Keep social services papers.
  • Ask the associations and NGOs where you take part or are a member for certificates.
  • Keep proof of your trips, of when you leave and enter Spain.
  • Ask for the criminal record certificate from your country in good time.
  • Check that your papers from other countries are valid, translated and have an apostille.

Keeping everything in order avoids delays.

12. The 2026 regularisation is over

In 2026 there was a special regularisation process (Royal Decree 316/2026). The deadline ended on 30 June 2026. You can no longer apply. It is not the same as the 5 types of arraigo in this guide, which you can apply for all year round. Royal Decree 316/2026 (BOE).

13. Advice from Ubuntu Canarias

Every case is different. Two people who have been in Spain for the same time may have different options.

Before you apply, ask someone to review your case. Reaching the time is not enough: you have to choose the right type of arraigo and prepare your papers well.

At Ubuntu Canarias we guide you with information and papers. We are not lawyers. For your specific case, get legal advice. If you have questions, write to us. You can also read our questions about immigration.

No one should go through a procedure without understanding what they are asking for, what they have to prove and what their rights are.
