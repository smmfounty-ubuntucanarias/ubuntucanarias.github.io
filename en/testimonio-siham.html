Testimony

Talking About Migration Means Talking About Our Youth

Siham Al-Halimi
Arabic teacher · Rabat, Morocco · 23 years old

A view from the other side of the sea

At Ubuntu Canarias we work with people who have already migrated, but we're also interested in hearing from those who, on the other side of the sea, think and speak about why people migrate in the first place.

Siham Al-Halimi is 23, lives in Rabat and works as an Arabic teacher. Drawing on her experience with young Moroccans, she shares a reflection on the reasons that push part of her country's youth to consider a future outside Morocco.

Her testimony isn't about a journey already made, but about the decisions that take shape before setting out: the lack of opportunity, the need for guidance, and the hope that a better future can also be built at home.

Listening to those who stay too

Siham's testimony reminds us of something we keep very present at Ubuntu Canarias: migration doesn't begin at the border, but much earlier, in the opportunities — or the lack of them — that every young person finds in their own country.

Investing in education, guidance and support for young people, both at home and at destination, is part of the same work: building futures where migrating is a free choice, not the only way out.

Editorial note. This testimony is published with the authorisation of its author, Siham Al-Halimi, an Arabic teacher in Rabat, Morocco. Its content reflects her personal experience and opinion.
