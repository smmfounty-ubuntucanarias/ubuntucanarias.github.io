Immigration questions: frequently asked questions

Ubuntu Canarias · Talento Sin Fronteras · Reviewed in November 2026

We created this page with the questions people ask us most often, in person, by email and on WhatsApp. The answers are short and in plain language, and they link to our guides when you want to know more.

!

Important: this is general information, not legal advice. Every case is different and the rules change. Before you submit any application, check your situation with a social organisation or a lawyer. Reviewed in November 2026.

First steps

Why is it so important to register at the town hall (empadronamiento)?

The padrón is the register of the people who live in a municipality. Registering gives you access to public healthcare, lets you enrol your children at school and apply for some kinds of support. The registration certificate is also one of the best proofs of how long you have lived in Spain, which is key for arraigo.

You can and should register even if you do not have papers. We explain it step by step in our registration guide.

Can I register if I live in a room or do not have a rental contract?

Yes. The padrón shows where you really live, not whether you have a contract. If you rent a room, you are usually asked for an authorisation from the owner or main tenant of the home. If you have no fixed address, the town hall's social services can process a special registration.

Each town hall asks for documents in its own way: check with yours first.

What is the NIE and what is it for?

The NIE (Foreigner Identity Number) is your personal number in Spain. You need it for almost every procedure: working, opening a bank account, paying taxes or signing a contract.

Be careful: having an NIE does not mean you have a permit to live or work in Spain. It is only an identification number.

What is the difference between the NIE and the TIE?

The NIE is the number; it never changes. The TIE (Foreigner Identity Card) is the physical card you get when you are granted a residence permit. It shows your photo, your NIE, the type of permit and an expiry date. When you renew, the card changes, but your NIE stays the same.

How do I get an appointment for immigration procedures?

Appointments are free and are booked on the official appointment website. You choose the province (Las Palmas) and the procedure.

Demand in Las Palmas is high and it can be hard to find a slot. Try on different days and at different times. If you have been trying for a long time, ask a social organisation for help.

Someone is asking me for money to get me an appointment. Is that legal?

Appointments are free. Nobody should charge you for an immigration appointment. If you are asked for money, do not pay and be wary of that person or website. You can report it to the Policía Nacional.

Can I get a free lawyer even if I do not have papers?

Yes, in many cases. Free legal aid is for people without sufficient means, and in procedures such as asylum or expulsion your immigration status does not matter. You apply at the Legal Guidance Service of the Bar Association.

We explain it in more detail in Can you get a free lawyer in Spain even without papers?

Which organisations can help me with an immigration procedure?

Some associations and foundations give guidance and support with immigration procedures, and some are listed in the Register of Immigration Collaborators, so they can submit applications on your behalf. Being registered does not mean they handle every procedure: always ask what they can help you with.

You will find them in our guide Directory of social services in Gran Canaria.

If I do not have papers, can I see a doctor?

Yes. Foreign people living in Spain without a residence permit have the right to public healthcare, with some requirements. You apply at your health centre with your passport (or another identity document), your registration certificate or other proof that you live here, and a signed declaration.

You do not have to wait for the answer to be seen: when you apply, you are given a provisional document. Emergencies are always attended.

Can my children go to school if we do not have papers?

Yes. All children and teenagers under 18 have the right to education in Spain, whether or not they have papers. Between the ages of 6 and 16, going to school is also compulsory. Schools cannot refuse enrolment because of the family's immigration status.

Asylum and international protection

What is international protection?

It is the protection Spain gives to people who have had to flee their country because their life, freedom or safety is in danger. For example, because of persecution for reasons of race, religion, nationality, political opinion, gender or sexual orientation, or because of a serious conflict.

You apply at the border or, if you are already in Spain, to the Policía Nacional. Before doing so, get advice: CEAR, the Red Cross or UNHCR can guide you.

Can I work while I wait for the answer to my asylum application?

Yes, but not from day one. If six months after submitting your application you still have not received an answer, you can work. This authorisation appears on your applicant document, known as the «red card».

3

Extraordinary regularisation

Is the extraordinary regularisation still open?

No. The deadline to apply ended on 30 June 2026. If someone tells you that you can still apply, be wary.

If you did not apply, today the ways to get papers are those of the immigration regulations, such as the different types of arraigo.

I submitted my application. What do I do while I wait?

Keep your proof of submission safe and check the status of your file from time to time. We explain it in Extraordinary regularisation: I have applied, what now?

It was granted. How long does it last and what rights do I have?

The authorisation lasts one year and allows you to live and work in Spain, as an employee or self-employed. When it ends, you have to move to another permit (we explain this in «Renewing or changing my permit»).

More information in Regularisation: key points and rights and in the quick guide to the authorisation.

4

Arraigo

What types of arraigo are there?

Under the immigration regulations in force since May 2025, there are five types:

Socio-labour (sociolaboral), if you have a job offer. Social, if you have family living in Spain or an integration report. Socio-training (socioformativo), if you are going to take a training course. Family (familiar), if you are the mother or father of, or look after, a child or a person with a disability who has Spanish or EU nationality. And second chance (segunda oportunidad), if you had a permit in the previous two years and lost it.

Each one has its own requirements. Before submitting anything, review your case with an organisation or a lawyer.

How long do I need to have lived in Spain to apply for arraigo?

In general, two years living in Spain continuously. Family arraigo does not require this period.

The permit you get usually lasts one year (five for family arraigo) and allows you to work. With socio-training arraigo, up to 30 hours a week.

For socio-labour arraigo, what contract do I need?

One or more contracts adding up to at least 20 hours a week, with pay equal to or above the minimum wage or the collective agreement. The company must meet certain requirements, such as being up to date with the tax office and Social Security.

How do I prove how long I have been in Spain?

The best proof is the historical registration certificate (certificado histórico de empadronamiento). If there are gaps without registration, other official documents with your name and a date can help: medical appointments or reports, course enrolments, documents from social services or organisations, etc.

Keep everything from day one, on paper and on your phone.

Do I have to apostille and translate my documents?

Almost always, yes. Documents from your country (such as a criminal record certificate or a birth certificate) must be apostilled or legalised, depending on the country. If they are not in Spanish, they need a translation, usually a sworn one.

The criminal record certificate is requested in your country or at its consulate, and it expires: check the date before you submit it.

5

Renewing or changing my permit

My regularisation authorisation lasts one year. What happens when it ends?

You have to move to an ordinary permit. The most common option is a four-year residence and work permit. In general, you need to have worked at least three months in the year and have a new contract, or to have been registered with Social Security for nine months within a twelve-month period. There are other routes, for example if you have family in Spain.

Check with an organisation or a lawyer which one fits your case.

I have not managed to work the three months. Do I lose my papers?

Not necessarily. If you cannot move to the four-year permit, there is an exceptional one-year extension. To apply, you must be registered as a job seeker and actively looking for work, or have a favourable integration report from the regional government.

With the extension you keep living and working legally for one more year. If you cannot work because of a serious illness, a disability or retirement age, the extension can last four years.

When do I have to apply for the renewal?

Check the expiry date on your card or on the decision. It is best to apply in the two months before it expires: that way your permit stays valid until you get an answer.

You can also apply up to three months after, but you may be fined. After that, the permit is extinguished. Do not leave it until the last minute.

My card has expired but I applied for the renewal on time. Can I keep working?

Yes. If you applied for the renewal within the deadline, your permit is extended until you get an answer and you can keep working. Keep your proof of submission: it is your proof for your employer.

More information in How can I renew my residence in Spain?

6

Travel

Can I travel to my country with my residence permit?

In general, yes, if your card and passport are valid. If your card has expired and you are renewing it, find out before you leave: you may need a return authorisation.

If you have international protection (or have applied for it), do not travel to your country without getting advice first: you could lose it.

Can I travel to other European countries with my card?

With your valid Spanish residence card and your passport, you can travel around the Schengen area for up to 90 days in any 180-day period, adding up all countries. It is for short trips: it does not allow you to live or work in another country.

Ireland and Cyprus do not apply the Schengen rules in the same way: check before you travel there.

If I leave Spain without papers, can I come back?

You are not guaranteed to be able to return. To enter, you will have to meet the entry requirements for your nationality, such as having a visa if one is required. If you have a pending procedure (such as asylum), leaving may harm it. Get advice before you leave.

7

Nationality

How many years do I need to apply for Spanish nationality?

It depends on your case. The general rule is 10 years of legal and continuous residence. It is 5 years if you have refugee status; 2 years if you are from an Ibero-American country, Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea or Portugal, or of Sephardic origin; and 1 year in some cases, such as being born in Spain or having been married to a Spanish citizen for a year.

Time without papers does not count. We explain it in Spanish nationality by residence: an easy guide.

Does the time with the regularisation authorisation or an extension count?

Yes. The time you live in Spain with a residence authorisation counts as legal residence, also during an extension or while a renewal submitted on time is being decided. What does not count is time on a student stay or time spent without papers.

What are the DELE and CCSE exams?

They are two tests generally required for nationality. The DELE A2 shows that you know basic Spanish; the CCSE is a test about the Constitution and Spanish society. Both are run by the Instituto Cervantes.

People from countries where Spanish is an official language do not have to take the DELE. Minors do not take either test, and there are exemptions or waivers in other cases.

Do I have to give up my original nationality?

It depends on the country. People from Ibero-American countries, Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea and Portugal can keep it, and France has an agreement with Spain. In other cases, Spain asks you to declare that you renounce your previous nationality.

That declaration does not always mean your country takes away your nationality: that depends on your country's law. If this applies to you, ask at your consulate.
