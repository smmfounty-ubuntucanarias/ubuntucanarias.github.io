The two pairs of glasses that taught me how to see the world

By Soraya Mohamedi Mohamed
Chairperson of Ubuntu Canarias · Psychologist, specialist in Migration and Social Inclusion

Voices of the World: stories in the first person

At Ubuntu Canarias we know that behind every migration journey there is a person with their own outlook, their own voice and their own way of telling it.

That's why we created Voices of the World: a space where those who have lived that experience — or, as in today's case, walked closely alongside it — can share it in their own words, without intermediaries, to build bridges between cultures.

No single story speaks for all the others. Each testimony is unique, with its own context and lessons, and it is precisely that plurality of voices that gives this project its meaning.

Today we share the testimony of Soraya Mohamedi Mohamed, chairperson of Ubuntu Canarias, who tells us about growing up between two cultures, between two shores, and how she learned to turn that double perspective into a bridge between worlds.

A perspective to keep building bridges

At Ubuntu Canarias we believe that listening to stories like Soraya's helps us understand that growing up between two cultures is not about living divided, but about having the chance to see the world through two different pairs of glasses.

Her testimony reflects a lesson that runs through an entire lifetime: learning to stop seeing one's own story as a division and discovering it as a richness instead, turning that double perspective into a bridge that helps other people find their way.

Listening to these voices invites us to look at migration and identity from a more human perspective, based on respect, mutual understanding and intercultural coexistence.

Editorial note. This testimony has been published with the authorisation of its author, Soraya Mohamedi Mohamed, chairperson of Ubuntu Canarias. The content reflects exclusively the personal experience and opinions of the person who signs it.
