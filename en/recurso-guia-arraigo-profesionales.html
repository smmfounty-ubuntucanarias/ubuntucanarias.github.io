Arraigo: a guide for professionals

Ubuntu Canarias · For professionals and organisations · 20 min read · Published on 27 November 2026

Technical guide for professionals and organisations who advise or support people applying for an arraigo permit: requirements for each type, exceptions, documents, processing and the points worth checking before submitting an application.

Who this guide is for. It is written for professionals and organisations: staff at social organisations, social services, employment guidance, adult education and volunteers trained in immigration matters. If you are the person who is going to apply, we recommend our guide in plain language: Arraigo explained simply.

Information reviewed on 27 November 2026. The information sheets of the Ministry of Inclusion, Social Security and Migration are the administrative reference and take precedence over this guide. The rules may change. This guide offers documentary and social guidance, not legal advice.

1. Before pointing towards arraigo: rule out other routes

Arraigo is not the only route to regularisation, and it is not always the most suitable one. Before assuming the person has to wait two years, check whether they could access other permits:

  • Residence for family members of Spanish nationals.
  • Residence for family members of European Union citizens.
  • Permits on humanitarian grounds.
  • Permits for victims of gender-based violence or human trafficking.
  • Permits for cooperation with the authorities or for other exceptional circumstances.

Some of these routes do not require a minimum period of residence. If there are signs that one of them may apply, the prudent step is to refer to legal advice.

2. Legal framework

  • Immigration Regulation, Royal Decree 1155/2024 (consolidated text, BOE).
  • SEM Instructions 1/2025 on arraigo.
  • Royal Decree 316/2026, as a historical reference only (see section 12).

The regulation provides for five ordinary types of arraigo:

  1. Second chance (segunda oportunidad).
  2. Social-employment (sociolaboral).
  3. Social (social).
  4. Social-training (socioformativo).
  5. Family (familiar).

The general duration of the permit is one year. Family arraigo lasts five years.

3. General requirements: what to check in the first interview

Exclusions

  • Citizens of the European Union, the European Economic Area or Switzerland.
  • Family members covered by the EU citizens' regime. They should be guided towards that regime.
  • Holders of another stay or residence permit, or people involved in an ongoing procedure for granting, extending, renewing or modifying a permit.
  • International protection applicants, both when applying and during processing.

Residence period

  • Two years of continuous residence immediately before the application, for all types except family arraigo.
  • Absences may not exceed 90 calendar days in total during that period. This rule comes from the SEM Instructions 1/2025, not from the literal text of the regulation. It is worth reconstructing every exit and entry with the person.
  • Time spent while an international protection application is being processed does not count until there is a final decision in the administrative and, where applicable, judicial route. Any asylum history requires individual review.

Other requirements

  • Being in Spain.
  • No criminal record in Spain or in the countries of residence during the five years before entry, for offences that exist under Spanish law.
  • Not posing a threat to public order, security or public health.
  • Not being listed as inadmissible in countries with an agreement with Spain.
  • Not being within the period of a non-return commitment.
  • Paying the fee.

4. The five types

A. Second chance

Requirements:

  • Having held a residence permit in the two years immediately before the application.
  • That permit was not granted for exceptional circumstances.
  • The lack of renewal was not due to public order, security or public health.

The regulation also covers situations linked to judgments, dismissals or acquittals. Those cases should be referred to legal advice.

Specific documents:

  • Decision granting the previous permit.
  • Proof of holding it and of its validity.
  • Documents on the lack of renewal, if any.

Reference: Information sheet 27.

B. Social-employment (sociolaboral)

Requirements:

  • Two years of residence.
  • One or more contracts adding up to at least 20 hours a week.
  • Pay equal to or higher than the minimum wage or the collective agreement salary, in proportion to the working hours.
  • Several contracts are accepted for seasonal work or when there are simultaneous activities for different employers.

The company must be up to date with the tax office and Social Security and have the means to fulfil the contract. Applications often run into trouble on the employer's side, so it is worth reviewing the company's documents before submitting.

Two points worth explaining to the person:

  • Provisional authorisation. From the moment the application is accepted for processing, the person is provisionally authorised to reside and work as an employee.
  • Social Security registration. If granted, registration must take place within one month of notification. Without it, the permit does not take effect.

Specific documents:

  • Signed contracts.
  • The company's tax number (NIF).
  • Deeds or articles of association to prove the signatory's authority.
  • The company's solvency (personal income tax, VAT, corporate tax returns or company employment record).
  • Professional qualifications, if the post requires them, and recognition if the profession is regulated.

It should not be confused with the 2026 extraordinary arraigo. The requirement for contracts of more than 90 days in a year belonged to that procedure.

Reference: Information sheet 29.

C. Social

There are two routes, with different requirements.

Family route. A relationship with a foreign national who holds a residence permit: spouse, registered partner or first-degree relatives in direct line. It requires financial means of 100% of the IPREM for the applicant and another 100% for the family member, that is, 200% in total, available in Spain. If the means come from self-employment, specific requirements apply.

Integration route. If there are no family ties, a favourable social integration report is needed, issued by the regional government or, where applicable, by the local council. The report assesses:

  • Length of residence at the address.
  • Financial means.
  • Participation in social and employment integration programmes.
  • Learning Spanish.
  • Social and cultural integration efforts.

Specific documents:

  • Marriage or registered partnership certificates and birth certificates, as applicable.
  • The family member's legal residence.
  • Proof of financial means.
  • Integration report, where applicable.

Reference: Information sheet 28.

D. Social-training (socioformativo)

Requirements:

  • Two years of residence.
  • Enrolment in or completion of an accepted course.
  • Favourable social integration report.

Accepted training:

  • Upper secondary education in authorised centres, in a full-time programme.
  • Complete training for grade C professional certificates, levels 1, 2 and 3.
  • In-person compulsory education for adults.
  • Training promoted by public employment services for occupations in the relevant catalogue.

Blended learning is accepted for certain courses if the minimum attendance percentage is met. Check the course before the person enrols: it is one of the most common mistakes.

Deadlines:

  • If enrolment has an official period, the application is submitted in the two months before that period starts.
  • If the permit is granted without proof of enrolment, there are three months from notification to submit it.
  • Failing to prove enrolment or to complete the training may lead to the permit being terminated.

Work: it allows employed work of up to 30 hours a week, with the proportional minimum pay.

Specific documents:

  • Integration report.
  • Enrolment, admission or proof of attending the course.
  • Written commitment, in the case of public employment service training.

Reference: Information sheet 30.

E. Family

No minimum residence is required. There are two situations:

  • Father, mother or guardian of a child who is a national of the EU, the EEA or Switzerland and lives in Spain, who has the child in their care and lives with them, or meets their parental obligations.
  • Family member who supports a person with a disability, or who needs support measures to exercise their legal capacity, of that nationality, provided the person is in their care and they live together.

Specific documents:

  • The child's birth and nationality certificate, or guardianship document.
  • In the support situation, proof of the disability or need for support, of the relationship, of the care and of living together.
  • Identity document of the EU, EEA or Swiss citizen.

Reference: Information sheet 31.

5. Common documents and review points

Passport. Full copy, valid.

Residence. The historical municipal register certificate (padrón histórico) is the main evidence. To cover gaps, the following can be used:

  • Public healthcare visits or hospital stays.
  • Course enrolments and certificates.
  • Social services documents.
  • Any document from a public authority with name and date.

The Ministry prefers documents issued or registered by Spanish authorities. Go through the timeline month by month with the person and mark any gaps.

Criminal records.

  • They are required from the countries of residence in the five years before entry, apostilled or legalised, with a sworn translation if not in Spanish.
  • The certificate from a third country is not needed if five years of continuous residence in Spain immediately before the application can be proven.
  • Nor if it was already provided in a previous application within those five years and the person has not left Spain since then.
  • The Immigration Office checks Spanish records automatically.

In case of any criminal record, judicial investigation, conviction or police incident, refer to legal advice before submitting.

Form and fee. Form EX-10 is used for all five types. The fee is form 790, code 052, section 2.3.1, paid within ten working days after submission. If the application is submitted through MERCURIO, follow the platform’s instructions for the selected procedure.

6. Submission

  • In person, at the Immigration Office of the province of residence, with an appointment.
  • Online, through MERCURIO, with a digital certificate or through a representative.

7. Processing and follow-up

Requests for documents. The Office may ask for defects to be corrected within a set period. If this is not done correctly and on time, the file may be closed as withdrawn. It is worth agreeing with the person how they will let you know if they receive a request.

Decision. The deadline is three months. Silence means refusal, without prejudice to the relevant appeals. In case of an express refusal or refusal by silence, refer to legal advice to assess an appeal.

Notifications.

  • Check the processing status and electronic notifications with the person.
  • An electronic notification may be considered rejected if it is not accessed within ten calendar days.
  • If the notification cannot be delivered, it may be published in the Tablón Edictal Único (official notice board).

After the permit is granted.

  • Apply in person for the TIE within one month of notification.
  • For social-employment arraigo, Social Security registration within one month.

Work. The permit allows employed and self-employed work anywhere in Spain and in any occupation. For social-training arraigo, employed work only and up to 30 hours a week. For social-employment arraigo, provisionally from the moment the application is accepted for processing.

8. Extension and modification

  • Second chance, social-employment and social. Extended for one-year periods, proving active job search and registration as a jobseeker. There are justified exceptions: illness, disability or retirement age.
  • Social-training. Depends on continuing the studies or, if the training has finished, on proving the qualification or certificate and active job search.
  • Modification. It can be changed to a residence and work permit when its requirements are met.

It is advisable to plan this step with the person several months before the permit expires.

9. Canary Islands: the integration report

In the Canary Islands, the report is processed by the Government of the Canary Islands. Its regional information provides for training on constitutional values, human rights, democracy and equality, with exceptions. It also values learning Spanish, taking part in social, employment and cultural programmes, volunteering and taking part in networks and associations. Before advising, check on its website the hours required, the exceptions and the current way to apply.

  • Government of the Canary Islands: integration reports
  • Integration effort report (online office)
  • Social arraigo report (online office). The page shows an old deadline, so the general page should be checked.

Immigration Office of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria: Plaza de la Feria, 24, 35003. Official page.

10. The role of organisations: participation certificates

Taking part in associations and NGOs can prove integration efforts as supporting evidence. Joining an association is a right of all foreign nationals, including those in an irregular situation, so it is worth encouraging participation from the start.

If your organisation issues certificates, they should include:

  • The person's full name.
  • Name and details of the organisation.
  • Activity carried out.
  • Dates of participation.
  • Hours of training or collaboration, where they can be proven.
  • Signature and stamp.

It is important not to create false expectations. These certificates do not guarantee a favourable report or replace the official requirements.

11. Checklist for reviewing an application

  • Have other, more suitable residence routes been ruled out?
  • Is the person excluded for being an EU citizen or for having another permit or procedure in progress?
  • Do they have, or have they had, an asylum application? How does it affect the time count?
  • Can two years be proven without gaps? Do absences add up to less than 90 days?
  • Which criminal record certificates are needed and which can be omitted?
  • Are foreign documents valid, apostilled or legalised and translated?
  • Is the chosen type the right one?
  • For social-employment arraigo, is the company up to date and able to prove solvency?
  • For social-training arraigo, is the course accepted and are the enrolment deadlines met?
  • Has the integration report been requested in good time, if needed?
  • Does the person know how to follow their file and what to do if they receive a request?
  • Do they know the deadlines for the TIE and, where applicable, Social Security registration?

12. The 2026 extraordinary measures

Royal Decree 316/2026 introduced two extraordinary routes, for certain international protection applicants and for people in Spain before 1 January 2026. The deadline ended on 30 June 2026. They should not be confused with the ordinary types or presented as open.

13. A recommendation from Ubuntu Canarias

Two people who have been in Spain for the same time may have different options depending on their family, work, training and administrative circumstances. Supporting an arraigo application well is not just about gathering papers. It is about identifying the right type, anticipating the weak points of the file and knowing when to refer to legal advice.
