How to find a job in the third sector in Gran Canaria

Ubuntu Canarias · Talento Sin Fronteras · 8 min read · Published on 8 November 2026

The third sector is made up of associations, foundations, NGOs and cooperatives that work with people: children, older people, people with disabilities, migrants or homeless people. In Gran Canaria they publish job offers throughout the year, mainly in social education, care and job placement. How often depends on each organisation and its projects.

Between August and November 2026, our job board collected offers from 63 social organisations on the island. This guide sums up what those offers ask for, which organisations published the most, where to look and, at the end, a directory with more organisations on the island.

Ubuntu Canarias does not manage these offers or take part in the selection processes. We share each organisation’s official links.

Which profiles are in demand

  • Social educator and educational support worker (ATE). Mainly in child protection centres, foster flats and family programmes. Some residential services have night or weekend shifts.
  • Home care assistant and care worker for older people (gerocultor/a). Care for older or dependent people, at home or in centres.
  • Job placement, guidance or social integration technician. Supporting people in their job search or integration process.
  • Social worker. In children’s centres, housing, reception and disability services.
  • Intercultural mediator. Here, your native languages really count. Recent offers from Fundación SAMU and Asproinfa asked for Arabic, Pular, Wolof or Mandinka.
  • There are also jobs in administration, accounting, human resources, maintenance, cleaning and kitchens within the organisations themselves.

Which qualifications they ask for

In this sector, qualifications matter more than in others. These are the ones that come up most often in the offers, although the requirements of each specific offer always come first:

  • Degree in Social Education, Social Work or Psychology.
  • Higher Technician in Social Integration or Sociocultural Animation.
  • TCAE (nursing care assistant), Technician in Care for Dependent People (TAPSD) or the professional certificate in social and health care, for care and home help.

If your qualification is from another country, you may need to have it recognised. It depends on the qualification and the job: not all of them require it. We explain it in our guide Recognising a foreign qualification in Spain.

Requirements worth having ready

  • Certificate of no sexual offences. It is required by law for jobs involving regular contact with children, not for every job in the sector. You can get it free of charge on the Ministry of Justice website.
  • Driving licence and, sometimes, your own vehicle. Mainly asked for in home care and in programmes that cover several municipalities, not in every job.
  • Proven experience. Volunteering and work placements count if you put them on your CV with dates, organisation and tasks.
  • Languages. Put them on your CV even if they are not asked for: in mediation and reception they can make the difference.

If you want support with your job search

Some organisations have programmes that support you in your search: guidance, CV, interviews and training. They are not job offers but services to help you find one. Ask first whether places are available.

Where offers are published

  • Each organisation’s website. This is where most offers in the sector appear, and sometimes only there.
  • Hacesfalta.org, the third sector’s job and volunteering portal: hacesfalta.org
  • Canary Islands Employment Service: gobiernodecanarias.org/empleo
  • InfoJobs, Indeed and LinkedIn: search by the organisation’s name or for «educador social», «integración social» or «mediador intercultural» in Las Palmas.
  • Every week, our job board for Gran Canaria.

Tips for your application

  • Adapt your CV to each organisation: read what it does and who it works with, and mention any experience you have with that group.
  • If you have no paid experience, volunteering lets you gain experience, learn how an organisation works and make contacts. It does not guarantee a contract, but it adds to your CV.
  • Keep your qualification, the sexual offences certificate and your course certificates handy as PDFs, because you may be asked for them on the spot.
  • Send speculative applications to the organisations that interest you most, even if they have no offer open that day.

Never pay to get a job. Always use each organisation’s official channels and be wary if you are asked for money, bank passwords or details that are not relevant.

Ubuntu Canarias shares resources and job offers to make it easier to access work opportunities. We are not an employment agency and we do not act as intermediaries in hiring.
